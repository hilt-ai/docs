The triage queue for behavioral findings. Each finding represents a deviation detected by the anomaly scoring system (or raised by a detection rule); this page is where you review them and mark each one as a real anomaly or a false positive.

---

## Page header

The page header includes a **host selector** to scope findings to specific hosts, and **time presets** (5m, 15m, 1h, 6h, 24h, 7d) to control the time window.

When no finding is deep-linked, the page **auto-selects the first finding in the Review tab**.

## Filters

Filtering is structured through the **Filters** popover:

| Section | Options |
| --- | --- |
| **Type** | All · Activity spike · Network · Sensitive file · Lateral movement · Detection rule |
| **Severity** | All · Critical · High · Medium · Low |
| **Source** | All · Detection rules · ML anomalies |
| **Sort by** | Time · Severity · Confidence · Urgency |
| **Visibility** | A **Suppressed** toggle to reveal findings hidden by prior feedback |

Active filters appear as removable chips beneath the header. When you arrive scoped to a detection rule, a **Rule:** {name} chip shows the active scope.

## Finding queue

The queue is split into three tabs by review state:

| Tab | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| **Review** | Findings awaiting a verdict |
| **Accepted** | Findings you confirmed as real |
| **Denied** | Findings you dismissed |

### Finding cards

Each finding is a card showing:

- **Headline**: the finding's narrative headline (or its behavior label).
- **Relative time** and short **hostname**.
- **Type**: e.g. _Type: Activity spike_ (when classified).
- **Verdict**: an **Accepted** or **Denied** badge once reviewed.
- A one-line **summary** of what happened, with inline event chips.
- A **cluster** badge when the behavior is atypical for the host.
- **User / Reviewer** and **Confidence** (High / Medium / Low).
- A **Host quarantined · View** link when the host has been isolated.

The selected card is highlighted with a green accent. A **severity badge** (Critical, High, Medium, Low) is shown on each card. When available, **Time to Detect (TTD)** is also displayed (e.g. "detected ~30s after it began").

## Finding detail

Selecting a finding opens its report in the right panel. (If nothing is selected, the panel prompts you to _"Select a finding from the queue to view details."_) The report has two parts.

### Narrative

The top band explains the finding:

- **Headline**, with the **host** and finding **time**.
- A **type** badge and a **confidence** badge (hover for "Model Confidence").
- A **Quarantined** link when applicable.
- The narrative itself — _what happened_ and _why it's unusual_ — plus context chips.
- **Identity** badges for the job, application, or workload involved.

Two context banners may appear: a **workload phase change** note (explaining that volume-only signals were de-emphasized), and a **feedback-suppression** note when the pattern matches a prior finding a reviewer denied, including the pattern similarity (an exact match or a percentage).

### Reviewing a finding (Confirm / Dismiss)

In the narrative band you act on the finding:

- **Confirm**: mark it as a real anomaly (moves it to **Accepted**).
- **Dismiss**: mark it as benign or a false positive (moves it to **Denied**).
- A notes field (_"Add review notes..."_) lets you record context.

<Note>
**Read-only users** (Viewer role) do not see the Confirm/Dismiss buttons or the notes field. These HITL controls are only available to users with review permissions.
</Note>

### Action buttons

Two buttons in the detail panel provide quick next steps:

- **Explain**: launches a guided walkthrough of the finding, stepping through the narrative, evidence, and context to help you understand what happened.
- **Investigate**: opens a scoped event timeline on the [Investigate](/dashboard/investigate) page, pre-filtered to the finding's host and time window.

### Causal Graph

Below the narrative, the **Causal Graph** visualizes the chain of events that led to the anomaly. It is a vertical causal story graph with expandable process steps — each node shows what a process did and its downstream branch effects. Expanding a step reveals the specific actions (file accesses, network connections, child processes) that occurred at that point in the chain.
